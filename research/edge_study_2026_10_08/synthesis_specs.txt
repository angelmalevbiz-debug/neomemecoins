######## STRUCTURAL_RUG_GUARD_V1: a defensive filter, not a source of profit
A pure pre-entry rule that fails closed.

**Where it runs:** before quoting, before flow promotion and before the RugCheck call. It goes in backend/cost_first_established.rejections, the one place shared by the cost-first engine profile, both COST_FIRST Lab books and the scheduler's GROUP_COST_FIRST, and in every other entry path.

**Inputs:** liquidityUsd, marketCap (fall back to fdv), and pair age from pairCreatedAt.

**Reasons recorded in describe()**, checked in this order:
1. rug_input_unknown: any input missing, non-finite or <= 0. Stop here.
2. rug_lp_pullable: liq/mcap >= 1.0.
3. rug_young_pool: age < 720 min.
4. rug_fake_market_cap: mcap >= $20M AND liq/mcap < 1% AND age < 14 days.

**Recommended conservative additions.** Blocking more costs nothing in expectation because no strategy has an edge.
- Raise the fake-market-cap liq/mcap threshold to 2%. This catches DAWS-like pools; it was informed by the holdout and is not validated.
- Use a corrected ticker-reuse rule. Block a token younger than 14 days whose normalized ticker was already seen on another pair with a DIFFERENT mint. The interim rule counts a token's own pump.fun curve pair as a reuse, which is wrong.

**Naming:** call it something distinct from engine_rug_guard.py's RUG_GUARD_V2. That RugCheck-based check never ran on these families (checked_at 0) and passed SharkTank, which later collapsed.

**Companion fix:** in market_monitor.score_pair, limit the +9 'good liquidity/MC' bonus to 0.15 <= liq/MC < 0.6 and treat liq/MC >= 1 as a risk. Today, pools whose creator can pull the liquidity score 86.7 on average and sit at SETUP on 96.9% of rows.

**Reference code:** rug/rug_guard.py, frozen with sha256 20268e97..., and harness_final.rug_guard_v1.
HOLDOUT: **Drain census (22.8 h):**
- 157 terminal drains among 1,549 PumpSwap SOL pools.
- All 19 drains in pools that ever had >= $250k liquidity came from two structural families: 9 LP-pullable (liq/mcap 1.2-1.9, liquidity goes to 0 at an unchanged price, tickers recycled) and 10 fake market cap ($23M-$1.3B market cap with liq/mcap 0.25-1.9%).
- The other drains were young pools (median age 12.8 min).

**Rule thresholds were frozen on train after 8,960 configurations. Holdout results:**
- Drain-event recall 0.897 at a point false-positive rate of 0.148 (liquidity >= $20k).
- Drain hazard per position-hour: 4.39% to 1.31% with the guard; cost-first universe 3.48% to 0.99%; fee 100-125 bps with liquidity >= $250k went from 54%/h to fully blocked.
- Random entries on all PumpSwap pools, calibrated stressed net50: -8.72%/trade without the guard, -5.12% with it. Still a loss.

**Pre-registered forward test (1.92 h):**
- Flagged minute-samples lost >= 50% within 60 min in 10.8% of cases, against 1.7% for samples that passed.
- By reason: LP-pullable 49%, young pool 15.4%, fake market cap 0% (no family pool drained in this short window).
- 30-min stressed net: -9.25% flagged, -4.19% passed.

**Forward cost-first universe:**
- The guard flags 49% of minute-units; the interim screen flags 77%.
- Half of the trades the guard lets through were still in DAWS-like family pools that pass the 1% threshold: USDF EpugLBw1, UDR ERFmQZQU, GOIF 8ZMkMgWM, DOTF 5GaCcKLd and WOTF HJ72PgqL. That is why the 2% and corrected-ticker additions are recommended.
VERDICTS: - **Rug study:** works as a risk filter; 0 profit candidates.
- **Leaderboard:** removes about 3.6 percentage points per trade of loss on random entries; no guarded row passes the gate.
- **Forward check:** 'consistent' on both pre-registered outcomes. The direction matches the prediction, but the pair-bootstrap CIs touch 0: catastrophic-loss rate difference +9.1 pp, CI [-1.8, +22.4]; 30-min difference -5.1 pp, CI [-12.7, +0.7].
- **Honest caveat:** in this 1.9 h window no fake-market-cap pool drained. The cost-first trades the guard removed actually did better (-1.1% vs -2.7%) because the scam pools drift up on wash buys until they are drained. The guard's value is in the drain tail, about -100% per event (10 of the 64 pools that entered the cost-first universe drained within 22.8 h), and a 2-hour window cannot show that.

######## HEAT_VETO_STACK: a defensive entry filter
Block a new entry in every book, and log the reason, when any of these holds at decision time. All inputs are fields the engine already has.

- (a) 5-min price return >= +3%, measured against the price 300 s earlier.
- (b) DexScreener 5-min buy share b5/(b5+s5) >= 0.70.
- (c) Volume acceleration v5/(v1h/12) >= 1.3.

Optional exit trigger, to run log-only first: sell a held token when it makes its first paid-profile appearance.

Lab arms that test a surge hypothesis (LAB_A) log these flags but do not apply them.

**Pre-registered forward test (1.92 h):** universe liquidity >= $20k, fee >= 55 bps, passing the structural guard.

######## Calibrated acceptance basis (methodology for engineers)
**Judge every book on net50**, which is the sum of:
- the engine cost model;
- CALIB_V1 extra cost per leg: fee <= 50 bps +22 bps; fee 55-125 bps a +10 bps floor; +25 bps outside the measured range (liquidity < $50k or trade size > 0.3% of liquidity); plus engine rent and network costs, about 5.5 bps/leg at $200;
- +50 bps/leg stress;
- +200 bps on stop exits and +100 bps on trailing exits.

leaderboard/harness_final.py implements this basis and the audited fills: next DexScreener refresh, feed-gap closes, survivorship-safe forward_net, drained pools valued at 0.

PAPER fills must come from executable quotes, never from DexScreener prints.
HOLDOUT: - Fee tiers and the 2N/L impact formula match Jupiter quotes to within about 0.1% (117 trades).
- The old harness was optimistic by 0.56-0.76%/trade because DexScreener prints lag the pool by about 20 s.
- Stop exits fill about 2% worse than the model.
- The +50 bps stress covers the average gap but only 60% of individual trades.
- The audit found two artifacts: zero-latency stale fills, and survivorship in forward_net (fee 100-125 bps with liquidity >= $250k showed +7.69% at 60 min; the real figure is -46%).
VERDICTS: - **Calibration and audit:** adopted by the integrator.
- **Effect on conclusions:** none. Every leaderboard row is negative on the model-cost, plain-stress and calibrated bases alike.

######## PROMOTION GATE and run protocol (applies to every Lab test)
**How to run each hypothesis**
- As a PAPER Lab book.
- As a zero-capital shadow log of every signal: no slot limit, with the engine's executable quote recorded at the decision.
- Next to its random control book.
- From the frozen config hash in synthesis/strategies.py, with no re-tuning.
- Re-score it daily with leaderboard/run_all.py on the new scan log.

**Promote to an engine account only when ALL of these hold** (on trades entered after the Lab start):
- n >= 150 closed trades over >= 25 pairs and >= 3 days covering every UTC hour.
- Mean net50 > 0, and the pair-bootstrap CI95 lower bound of mean $/trade > 0. Net50 here = booked executable-quote P&L minus 50 bps per leg, minus 200 bps on stop exits.
- It beats the same-period random control by more than the control's CI half-width.
- top_pair_share <= 0.20, it stays positive with the best pair removed, and no single day supplies more than 50% of the P&L.
- A 3-slot $1000 book has a max drawdown of 20% or less.
- It made zero entries on pools flagged by the rug guard.
- Vanished or unpriced closes are under 10% of trades.

**Kill rule:** after at least 50 trades, stop if the mean net50 is below 0 and the CI95 upper bound is below 0, or if drawdown exceeds 30%.
RATIONALE: 22.8 hours of data can only detect effects of 2-3.5% per trade.
- Confirming a +2%/trade edge needs about 240-800 out-of-sample trades; +1% needs about 1,000-3,200.
- The old gate (20 trades with a positive mean) passes a strategy whose true mean is -1%/trade 34% of the time. With 3 configs per family, about 70% of families would show at least one false candidate.
- The 15-minute view the owner is watching is pure noise: per-trade standard deviation is 9-13%.

######## FOUNDATION audit
I audited the shared backtest harness that every research family relies on. The cost model is correct, and the decision path has no look-ahead leak for signals that only look at their own pair. But I found realism and survivorship bugs that change some of the established facts. All confirmed bugs are fixed in audit/harness_v2.py, which has the same API, uses the same series.pkl cache and needs no rebuild.

Writing REPORT.md was blocked by the harness (subagents must return findings as text), so the full report is in the findings and recommendations below.

**Look-ahead tests (passed for per-pair signals):**
- Brute-force checks of P(), ago and window: 0 mismatches.
- The ticker index used by the rug screen: 0 mismatches.
- A "poisoned future" test, where every value after a cut time T* is scrambled: decisions, exit_fn calls and trades closed before T* were identical.
- Two leaks do exist, both fixed in v2:
  - `P.static('price')` returns the whole column, future included.
  - simulate runs pair by pair, so any state a signal keeps across pairs sees other pairs' future (118,272 of 130,986 calls came after a call at a later time).

**Cost model:** identical to the engine's `paper_market_feasibility.modeled_roundtrip` on 135,512 raw rows (0 differences, 0 fee-tier mismatches).

**Main realism bug: stale fills.** The `upd` column is the engine's batch time (37,771 distinct values over 3.1M rows), not DexScreener's per-pair update time. The engine polls every ~5 s, but DexScreener refreshes a pair only every ~30 s, so 90% of kept points repeat the previous price exactly. v1 therefore filled entries at the signal's own price 86-89% of the time and exits at the trigger price 94-96% of the time. That means zero effective latency, and one take-profit filled at +432% on a single-print spike.
- Against the on-chain tape, DexScreener's price is the last trade from about 20 s earlier. It also bounces about ±50 bps between buy and sell prints.
- v2 fills at the next DexScreener refresh (the first later point whose price differs, within 60 s). Fill error against the tape drops from 188 to 114 bps, and most of the context-dependent bias disappears.

**Survivorship in forward_net.** It silently dropped samples whose pair left the feed (13.5% of 60-minute samples, mostly drained pools). Fact #1's 60-minute numbers change as follows:
- fee100-125 & liq>=250k: **+7.69% becomes -46.1%** (sample count 333 becomes 706).
- fee<=50 & liq>=250k: -0.49% becomes -4.04%.
- fee100-125 & liq50-250k: +0.47% becomes -5.18%.

**Fact #2 (random entries lose) stands under v2.** Holdout mean per trade at the stressed basis:

| universe | v1 | v2 |
|---|---|---|
| all PumpSwap | -7.68% | -7.20% |
| cost-first | -5.97% | -5.59% |
| cost-first + interim rug guard | -3.30% | -2.98% |

The rug guard's benefit is overstated. Its thresholds were chosen after seeing 7 rugs that drained in the holdout window. A guard built only from the 3 train-window rugs gives -4.61% instead of -2.98%.

**smoke3.py run unchanged under each harness:**

| run | v1 train / holdout | v2 train / holdout |
|---|---|---|
| no guard | -3.68 / -5.97 | -3.32 / -5.59 |
| interim guard | -4.78 / -3.30 | -2.24 / -2.98 |

None of the momentum or dip probes or the random baselines is near positive expectancy under either harness.

**The candidate gate is far too weak for 22.8 hours of data.**
- The smallest effect the holdout can reliably detect is 2-3.5% per trade.
- With 20 holdout trades, a config whose true mean is -1% shows a positive holdout mean about 34% of the time.
- When 3 configs are picked on train, roughly 70% of families would show at least one false candidate.

Credible claims need about 240-800 holdout trades for a +2%/trade edge, which means 1-3 more days of data.
- high Stale-quote fills: entries and exits filled at the decision price (zero effective latency)
    Cause:
- `upd` is the engine batch time (37,771 distinct values over 3.1M rows, ~82 rows each; t-upd median 1.2 s), not DexScreener's updatedAt.
- So the de-dup rule keeps one row per ~5 s scan. 89.3% of kept PumpSwap points repeat the previous price and 86.8% repeat price+liq+v5+mcap. Only 20k of 715k same-price rows carry new verified-flow data.
- DexScreener changes price every ~30 s (between changes: p10 26.5 s, p50 31.9 s, p90 68 s).

Effect in v1:
- Entry at i+1 had the same price as the signal 86-89% of the time.
- Exit at k+1 had the trigger's price 94-96% of the time, so gap risk was barely modeled.
- TP fills reached +432% on single prints. HOLD exits overshot by up to 87.5 min (p90 18.9 min).

Tape (113 pools, mid = sqrt(last buy x last sell)):
- Fill MAE vs truth at decision+2 s: v1 188 bps; v2 next-refresh rule 114 bps.
- Conditional mean bias after a >2% DexScreener up-move: v1 -66 bps (fill too high), v2 -6. After a >2% down-move: v1 +15 (median +26), v2 -12.
- Trade level, v1: STOP exits -0.6..-1.5% pessimistic; TP exits +0.3..+4.8% optimistic.

Fixed in v2 (F1): fill at the first later point whose price differs, within 60 s, else i+1. Afterwards the trigger->fill gap is p50 25-30 s, HOLD overshoot p90 0.7-0.8 min, and max TP fill +27.9%. Impact on config holdout means: -0.9..+1.1%/trade.
- high forward_net survivorship bias inflated base rates (established fact #1 is wrong in several buckets)
    - v1 returned None when the pair had no point after the horizon although the dataset continued: 8,307 of 61,475 60-min samples (13.5%).
- Of 1,549 PumpSwap pairs, 1,470 leave the feed more than 10 min before the end. Their last liquidity is p10 $20, median $20k, so many were drained.
- v2 (F3) values them at last price -10% (VANISHED) or by the gap rule. It returns None only when right-censored at the dataset end.

60-min forward net, v1 -> v2:
- fee100-125 & liq>=250k: +7.69% (n 333, win 74.8%) -> -46.11% (n 706, median -13.4, p10 -100%)
- fee<=50 & liq>=250k: -0.49 -> -4.04 (median 0.01 -> -0.46)
- fee100-125 & liq50-250k: +0.47 -> -5.18
- fee100-125 & liq<50k: -14.55 -> -21.00
- fee55-95 & liq50-250k: -4.03 -> -5.58
- fee55-95 & liq>=250k: -2.95 -> -3.08

Survivorship plus the liq==0 fix alone (v1 fills) gives -46.15 for the first bucket, so the change is survivorship, not the fill rule.
- high Candidate gate (holdout n>=20, mean>0) is statistically meaningless on 22.8 h
    Inputs:
- Per-trade stressed sd: 9.1% (mid-fee random) to 13.2% (all PumpSwap random).
- Pair-cluster design effect: 0.7-2.4.
- Minimum detectable effect at 80% power, holdout: 2.0-3.6% per trade.

False-pass risk under a true mean of -1% (sd 9%, design effect 1.5):
- P(holdout mean > 0) = 34% at n=20, 26% at n=50, 13% at n=150.
- With 3 configs pre-selected on train, P(at least one false pass) = 72% / 60% / 35%.

Holdout trades needed for 80% power: +3%/trade 106-353; +2% 238-795; +1% 950-3,200.

The span is one 22.8 h regime; train and holdout cover different times of day. Random all-PumpSwap hourly means range from -15.6% to -2.9%. 3.2% of train trades exit after the cut (no embargo).

This cannot be fixed in code; see recommendations.
- medium Cross-pair look-ahead: pair-by-pair simulate lets global state in signal/exit_fn see other pairs' future
    - v1: 118,272 of 130,986 signal calls happened after a call at a later time. Any cross-sectional or regime state kept inside a signal is leaky.
- v2 (F5): a heap scheduler over per-pair generators invokes every callback (signal, size_fn, exit_fn) in global non-decreasing time order.
- Result: 0 inversions. Trades are identical to pair order for stateless signals (3 configs verified). About 25% slower.
- Custom `run(H)` simulators must enforce time order themselves.
- medium Past.static() leaks the full future column
    - v1 `P.static('price')` returned the whole array, so `P.static('price')[P.i+1]` was possible.
- v2 (F4) raises LookAhead for any time-series column. Static keys (pair, mint, dex, quote_sol, sym, created) still work.
- `P.s` remains reachable in Python. Integrators should grep strategy modules for `P.s[`, `.s['` and `P.i +`.
- medium Feed gaps while holding: exit at reappearance price, hold limits overshot, tails selected
    v1 behavior:
- 66 of 1,057 random trades sat through a >5 min feed absence (gap median 53 min, p90 111 min). They exited at the reappearance price, and stops/targets could not act during the gap.
- Those trades averaged +2.16% vs -9.68% for the others.

Gap returns vs matched continuously observed controls over the same interval:
- median +0.5% vs 0.0%
- p90 +25% vs +7.6%
- share below -50%: 4.8% vs 2.2%
- liq>=250k gaps: +1.4% median, no drains

v2 (F2):
- Absence over 10 min closes the trade, valued at min(pre-gap, return price). This is conservative: drains during a gap are kept and re-listing pumps are not credited.
